You are a product designer for a GST compliance SaaS in India. Map the follow-up flow for one supplier, from the moment reconciliation flags an invoice until the credit is confirmed or lost. Use only the facts and rules below. Return:
1. The states, each with a one-line definition.
2. Every transition, as "from → to: what causes it (who or what)".
3. The steps where a person must decide, and why.
4. Two edge cases this flow handles badly.
Keep each line under 15 words. Do not add rules that are not listed. Answer in under 350 words.

FACTS
- Reconciliation compares the buyer's purchase register with GSTR-2B and flags invoices the supplier has not reported correctly.
- Only the supplier can fix it, by reporting or amending the invoice in their GSTR-1, due on the 11th.
- IMS shows the buyer what a supplier filed within days; GSTR-2B, generated on the 14th, confirms it.
- FY 2025-26 credit must appear in GSTR-2B by 14 Nov 2026 to be claimed before 30 Nov 2026.
- Suppliers reply by email or WhatsApp, in English, Hindi, Hinglish, Gujarati or Marathi.

RULES
R1 A clear promised date more than a week away: one reminder 2 days before it.
R2 An out-of-office reply with a return date: follow up the day after they are back.
R3 A dispute, condition, request for documents, unclear reply, wrong contact or hostile tone: a person decides.
R4 A frustrated or hostile tone: alert the relationship owner and slow reminders.
R5 A promised fix not in IMS after the GSTR-1 due date: mark the promise broken and draft a firmer message for approval.
R6 A supplier who wants payment first: a hold may be suggested, never for micro or small enterprises or invoices over 150 days old.
R7 A supplier who says it is already fixed: keep it open until it appears in GSTR-2B.
R8 FY 2025-26 credit: flag the 30 Nov deadline and put it first.
